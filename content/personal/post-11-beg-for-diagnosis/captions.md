# Post 11: Beg for Diagnosis

## Instagram Caption

I had to beg for a written diagnosis.

By the time FJ was two, I knew. I had three other kids before him. I knew what developmental milestones looked like. I knew something was different.

His pediatrician told me to wait. "Every kid is different." "Boys talk later." "Let's give it time."

But I couldn't wait. Early intervention has a clock on it. In California, the regional center provides incredible services for children on the spectrum, but you need a diagnosis to access them. And the earlier you start, the better the outcomes.

So while I was waiting for doctors to take me seriously, I started speech therapy on my own. Occupational therapy. Reading everything I could find. Spending money I didn't have because I refused to lose time I couldn't get back.

Eventually I got the written diagnosis. Not because anyone handed it to me, but because I kept showing up, kept asking, kept advocating until someone finally listened.

Here's what I want every parent in this position to know: You are not imagining it. Your instincts are valid. If you feel like something is different about your child's development, push for answers. Find another doctor if you have to. Get the evaluation. Document everything.

The system is not designed to hand you a diagnosis. It's designed to wait and see. But waiting costs time, and time is the one thing you can't get back.

If you're fighting for answers right now, keep fighting. Your child is worth it.
