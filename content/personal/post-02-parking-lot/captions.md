## 📱 FEED CAPTION (Instagram Post)

The day FJ was diagnosed, I drove to the parking lot of a grocery store I never go to and sat there.

I didn't cry. I wasn't ready for that yet. I wasn't angry. I wasn't relieved. I was just still. Like my brain needed to buffer before it could process what I'd just heard.

I looked at my phone. I almost Googled "autism prognosis." I put it down. I almost called my mom. I put the phone down again.

Eventually I drove home. FJ was with his grandma. He ran to the door when I walked in, same as always. Same smile. Same kid.

Nothing had changed about him. Everything had changed about how I understood him.

If you're in that parking lot right now - metaphorically or literally - you don't have to do anything. You don't have to have a plan. You just have to get home.
