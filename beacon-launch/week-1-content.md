## Value Post 1: "5 Things I Wish Someone Told Me"

**Caption (FB + IG):**

5 things I wish someone told me the week my child was diagnosed:

1. Therapy waitlists are 6-12 months long. Get on them NOW, even if you're not sure which therapy is right. You can always say no when they call.

2. Your pediatrician probably isn't the expert. Find specialists. Join parent groups. The real knowledge is in the trenches.

3. IEPs are negotiable. Everything in that document can be changed. You have more power than they want you to know.

4. Other autism parents will become your village. The friends who "don't get it" will fade. That's okay.

5. Your child is still the same amazing kid they were yesterday. The diagnosis didn't change them. It gave you a roadmap.

## Value Post 2: "IEP Meeting Tomorrow"

**Caption (FB + IG):**

IEP meeting tomorrow? Screenshot this.

Before you walk in:
□ Request the draft IEP 3 days early (your legal right)
□ Write down your top 3 priorities
□ Bring someone with you if you can

In the meeting:
□ Ask them to explain anything you don't understand
□ Take notes or record (check your state's laws)
□ You do NOT have to sign same-day

If they say "we can't do that":
□ Ask them to put the denial in writing
□ Request the specific policy or law they're citing
□ Say "I'd like to table this and revisit at a follow-up meeting"

You are the expert on your child. Don't let anyone make you forget that.

Save this. Share it with a parent who has a meeting coming up.

## Value Post 3: "Meltdown vs Tantrum"

**Caption (FB + IG):**

"They're just throwing a tantrum."

No. Here's the difference:

TANTRUM:
• Goal-oriented (wants something)
• Stops when they get what they want (or realize they won't)
• Aware of audience
• Can be redirected

MELTDOWN:
• Neurological overload
• NOT goal-oriented
• Cannot stop on command
• Not aware of or responsive to audience
• Needs safety, space, and time

A tantrum is a negotiation.
A meltdown is a nervous system crash.

You can discipline a tantrum.
You can only support a meltdown.

Understanding this changed everything for our family.

Share this with someone who needs to hear it.

#AutismMeltdown #Meltdown #AutismParent #AutismAwareness #SensoryOverload #AutismMom #AutismDad #Neurodiversity
